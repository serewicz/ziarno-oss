# Outline: Beginning AI with open-source tools

## By the end of the day, each person can
1. Explain what a language model is, and what "open weights" means.
2. Run a small open model on an ordinary laptop with no cloud account.
3. Choose a model size that fits the hardware they have.
4. Explain RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) and build a tiny version.
5. Name the risks: wrong answers, bias, privacy, and license terms.
6. Teach the first two labs to co-workers.

## Modules
1. **What a language model does.** It predicts the next word. Why it can sound right and still be wrong.
2. **Open models.** Open weights vs. open source. Model licenses differ: always read them. Where to find models (Hugging Face, Ollama library).
3. **Running models locally.** Ollama (easy) and llama.cpp (lighter, more control). Why local matters: privacy, cost, no outside dependence.
4. **Small models on cheap hardware.** Model size (parameters), quantization (smaller files that use less memory), and what fits in 4, 8, or 16 GB of memory.
5. **RAG basics.** Give the model your own documents to answer from, so answers are grounded and checkable.
6. **Using AI responsibly.** Checking answers, keeping private data local, and saying when AI is the wrong tool.
7. **Teach-back.**

## Hardware
Any laptop from the last several years with at least 8 GB of memory. 4 GB can work with the smallest models, slowly. No graphics card needed.
