# Module 10: Digital public goods and sovereignty

**Level:** Leader  
**Time:** about 3 hours  
**Needs:** a laptop or a phone with a browser; some exercises need an internet connection once.

## Learning objectives
By the end you will be able to:
- Explain digital public goods (DPGs) and the DPG Standard
- Find DPGs that could replace a closed tool
- Explain digital sovereignty without overselling it

## Lesson outline
1. What a DPG is: open, useful for development goals, does no harm
2. The DPG Standard and Registry
3. Digital public infrastructure: identity, payments, data exchange
4. Sovereignty: control of data, skills at home, choice of vendor
5. Real limits: open source still needs local skills and budget
6. Principles for Digital Development

## Hands-on exercise: Find a DPG for a real need
1. Open https://digitalpublicgoods.net/registry .
2. Use the search box to look for a need in your country, for example "health records" or "education".
3. Open two results. For each, note: license, who uses it, what languages it supports.
4. Open https://www.digitalpublicgoods.net/standard and check one result against the indicators.
5. Write half a page: the need, the DPG, what local skills it would take, first small step.

## Quiz
1. What makes something a digital public good?
2. Does open source alone give sovereignty?
3. Where can you find listed DPGs?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. It is open source (or open content/data), helps development goals, and meets the DPG Standard.
2. No. You also need local skills, budget, and control of data.
3. The DPG Registry.

</details>
